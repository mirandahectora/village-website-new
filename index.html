Group money is everywhere: family pools, savings circles, giving circles, club treasuries, mutual aid funds. Today it runs on one person's personal account, a group chat, and trust. Village replaces that with infrastructure: a shared balance, explicit rules, and a permanent record of every contribution, decision, and payout.

How Village works

Pick a mode. One ledger, either way.

  1. Create a pool.

    Set contribution amount and cadence, who can propose a payout, how the group decides, and where money can go.

  2. Pick a governance mode.

    Voting: payouts release when the group approves them. Rotating: the pool pays each member in turn on a fixed schedule. Manager: a designated admin approves releases within the group's rules.

  3. Fund it.

    Members link a bank account and contribute on schedule. Pooled balances sit at a regulated U.S. partner bank. Village runs the ledger and never holds member funds.

  4. Move money by the rules.

    An approved vote, a rotation turn, or a manager approval releases funds to the approved recipient: a member, a vendor, a nonprofit, or any destination the group named. Every contribution, vote, and payout is recorded and visible to every member.
